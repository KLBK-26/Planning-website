Day-of Coordination vs Partial Planning vs Full Planning: What's the Difference?

If you've started looking into wedding planning support, you've probably seen these three terms everywhere — and they're often used loosely, which makes it hard to know what you're actually paying for.

Day-of Coordination

This is for couples who've planned their whole wedding themselves and just want someone to run the day. It typically starts a few weeks before the wedding — final run sheet, vendor confirmations, and then a coordinator on the ground managing the timeline, the vendors, and any hiccups, so you're not doing it yourself.

Partial Planning + Coordination

This sits in the middle. You've booked some of the big vendors yourself, but you want help finishing the details, sourcing what's left, and getting proper guidance in the lead-up — plus everything included in day-of coordination. It usually starts several months out, with a handful of dedicated planning meetings along the way.

Full Wedding Planning

This is the whole thing, from concept to execution. Your planner builds the vision with you, sources every vendor, manages the budget from scratch, and coordinates the day itself. It starts the earliest — often 9 to 18 months out — because there's simply more to build.

How to know which one you need

Ask yourself how much of the wedding is already decided. If it's basically all booked, you probably want day-of coordination. If you've made a start but want it finished properly, partial planning fits. If you're starting from a blank page and want support the whole way, full planning is the one.
